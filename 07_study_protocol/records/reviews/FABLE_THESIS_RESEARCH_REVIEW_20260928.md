## 0. Executive summary (for the owner)

1. **The programme answers the proposal's question, and does so with more methodological rigour than most published federated-recommendation papers** (pre-registration, sealed selection, full-catalogue exact ranking over 158,486 items, a global chronological split, a hash-chained TEST register, paired user bootstrap on every contrast). Nothing that has been run needs to be re-run. **OPINION.**
2. **Three things weaken the answer to the question as posed, and all three are still fixable before the first downstream value is read:**
   - (a) The **"cloud" reference is a model trained on 9 % of the eligible users** (MAIN = 131,072 of 1,446,986; 2.59 M of 28.6 M supervised examples — MEASURED). Retention = arm / C_BIG therefore measures *"same-cohort federation cost"*, not *"what a real cloud model would have achieved"*. The registered-but-deferred **C_FULL** (one seed on the 1.32 M-user TIEBREAK band) is what turns the headline into an honest cloud-vs-device statement. I rank it **SHOULD (high)**, not optional.
   - (b) The federated arms use **one untuned local learning rate** (= the SMALL central peak) and **one untuned FedProx μ**. The federated-optimisation literature (Reddi et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2021 field guide) is explicit that client LR is the single most influential FL hyper-parameter. An untuned FA inflates the "federation cost" and an examiner will say so. A **pre-registered 2-point local-LR screen on CALIB (≈ 2–3 GPU-h, INFERENCE)** before any FL value is read is cheap insurance. **MUST** (it is the only cheap thing that can make the core number wrong).
   - (c) The **"privacy levels"** and **"bits vs quality"** contributions of the proposal are currently one DP point (ε = 8) and one dense-FP32 payload size. A privacy *curve* needs ε ∈ {∞, 8, 1} at least (ε = 1 is already first in the deferred tier — keep it there and treat it as core), and a bits-vs-quality *curve* needs at least one compressed-upload arm (8-bit quantised deltas) or it degenerates to "rounds × constant". **SHOULD.**
3. **The GRU-vs-SASRec contest is fair as a *procedure* but not robust as a *finding*:** the family order reverses between the two pre-registered checkpoint rules (PRACTICAL_BEST: SASRec +0.0043; CONTROLLED_BUDGET 6.0: GRU +0.0042; both CIs exclude 0 — MEASURED/REPORTED). The thesis must present the contest as "the backbone selected for the deployment study under rule T1", never as "SASRec beats GRU on REES46".
4. **Cold start (proposal Step 4)** is answered observationally (support-count bins), which confounds history length with user type. This is acceptable if worded as "retention as a function of available history", but a controlled-truncation panel (the registry already holds WARM_TRUNCATION_16384 and COLD_H_16384; only the E11.4 feature builder is missing) is the proper answer. **SHOULD (medium)**.
5. **Everything else** (persona/strata, kNN-emb and rule baselines, ONNX INT8, TEST once, disclosures) is in place or planned correctly. The write-up needs one framing device that the project has not yet written down anywhere: an explicit **privacy-level ladder** (cloud-train/cloud-infer → cloud-train/device-infer → FL → DP-FL → device-only) into which every arm is placed. That ladder *is* the proposal's "quantified tradeoff data for various privacy levels".

The remainder is organised as the owner asked: A alignment, B literature benchmark, C methodological audit, D what to redo/scale/add, E literal forward plan + thesis structure, F top-10 defence risks, G verdict.

### B.2 Where this project exceeds the bar (MEASURED, with the literature counterpart)

1. **Exact full-catalogue ranking over K = 158,486 with a pessimistic tie rule** (EVALUATION_CONTRACT §6.1–6.2, guard G2). Almost every FedRec paper in §B.1 uses 100 sampled negatives; Krichene & Rendle 2020 show sampled metrics are inconsistent even in expectation. This alone puts the evaluation above FedFast/PFedRec/FELLAS.
2. **Global chronological split** (TRAIN 1 Oct–15 Nov; VALIDATION 16–22 Nov; RETRO_TEST 23–29 Nov 2019; label grace 30 Nov; E1.1–E1.11) with 0 leaked labels audited (LABEL_TIME_AUDIT). Ji et al. 2023 and Gusak et al. 2025 show leave-one-out leaks the future; most FedRec papers use leave-one-out.
3. **Pre-registration with hashes before every read** (PREG1_LOCK_VALUES frozen before any central result; amendments 02–06A each "registered before … read"; G2 script hash frozen before unsealing; sealed run outputs by construction, `trainer.py:338` per DL 88). This is beyond the norm even at NeurIPS; it is the thesis's strongest methodological credential.
4. **A hash-chained TEST_EXPOSURE_REGISTER and a single TEST look at ALL_METHODS_LOCK** (§10 of the contract; register entries 1–4). No FedRec paper reports this.
5. **Paired user bootstrap with a shared resample matrix on every pre-registered contrast, ratios bootstrapped directly, per-seed reporting, claim vocabulary** (§7). Ihemelandu & Ekstrand 2021 find most RecSys papers report no inference at all.
6. **Scale of the federation**: 131,072 logical clients, 6,827 rounds of 64, 3 visits per client, 10 % drop-out, uniform-without-replacement cohorts of 1,024 for DP (participation.py L14–18). FedRec papers typically federate 6k (ML-1M) users with full participation. FedScale-style realism (drop-out) is present.
7. **Strong non-neural baselines** (POP, LAST, session-kNN k=500, kNN on cached embeddings, category/brand rule). Ludewig & Jannach 2018 make SKNN mandatory; it is here (0.11138 vs 0.161 for the winner, REPORTED DL 132).
8. **Deterministic FP32 training and evaluation with recorded numerics** (STRICT determinism receipts SYS-04; T3 config in the comparison key). Hidasi & Czapp 2023 and Petrov & Macdonald 2022 show implementation drift changes conclusions; here every run is bitwise reproducible.
9. **A user-disjoint participation-gap panel** (UNSEEN_VAL / UNSEEN_HOLDOUT, 32,768 users each), following Yuan et al. 2022 — rarely done in FedRec.
10. **A causal, on-device-computable feature contract** (USER_HISTORY_L50, session_offset, all channels classed LOCAL/SHARED; CLIENT_CONTRACT §6 lists exactly what pooled information every arm receives). Most FL papers never state what the "bootstrap block" is.

### B.3 Where this project falls short of the bar, or of the proposal (OPINION unless marked)

1. **Cloud reference = cohort model** (MEASURED: MAIN 131,072 / 1,446,986 users). Papers compare FL to a central model trained on *all* the data the FL system saw — which is what MAIN does (same cohort). But the proposal's "quality ceiling for centralized recommendations" implies the cloud's *data advantage*. Without C_FULL the thesis cannot say how much a real cloud model would beat the device. (Fix: §D-1.)
2. **No FL hyper-parameter tuning at all** (local LR = SMALL peak; μ = 0.01; server LR 1; local optimiser AdamW reset per visit). Reddi 2021 / Wang 2021 tune client LR on a grid for every FL baseline. (Fix: §D-2.)
3. **Two seeds and a fixed 6.0-EFE budget** for every arm. Common in RecSys (often one seed), but the FL literature reports rounds-to-target as well; our 12-mark curves give that for free — make sure the thesis reports "rounds to 90 % of C_SMALL" per arm.
4. **One dataset, one retailer, 46 days.** Normal for an MSc; must be in Threats to Validity. **INFERENCE worth stating:** the RETRO_TEST week [23 Nov, 30 Nov 2019) contains Black Friday (29 Nov 2019) — a seasonal shift relative to VALIDATION. Retention *ratios* should be more robust than absolute MRR to this shift, which is itself a small argument for the ratio design; report the VALIDATION→TEST drop of C_BIG alongside.

### C.6 FL simulation realism and the untuned knobs

- **MEASURED knobs:** 64 clients/round; 10 % seeded drop-out; 6,827 rounds (= 3 × 131,072 / 64 ÷ 0.9); B16 × 2 passes; n_consumed-weighted FedAvg; local AdamW reset every visit; server LR 1; local LR = SMALL peak × s(EFE) (exposure "end"); FedProx μ = 0.01; PF λ = 1e-4, personal LR = shared peak × round factor.
- **Realism (OPINION):** cross-device-like and above the FedRec norm; drop-out and cohort sampling follow FedScale/LEAF practice. Missing pieces common in systems papers (stragglers, heterogeneous compute, asynchrony) are out of scope and can be listed.
- **Untuned local LR — the main internal-validity risk of the whole downstream study.** With clients holding 5–100 examples and B16 × 2 passes, most visits make 1–13 optimiser steps; the "right" client LR for FedAvg is frequently *not* the central peak (Reddi 2021 Fig./Wang 2021 §4). If FA is under-trained at 6.0 EFE, R(FA) is biased downward and the thesis will "find" a large federation cost that is really an LR artefact. Mitigation is cheap and pre-registrable now (no FL value has been read): a 2-point screen {0.5×, 2×} vs 1× of the SMALL peak, FA only, CALIB cohort, 1.0 EFE (≈ 1/6 of a run each; INFERENCE ≈ 2–3 GPU-h total on an A40), rule = higher CALIB_VALIDATION macro MRR@20 at the 1.0-EFE mark, tie ≤ 0.0005 → 1×; the selected multiplier applies to FA/FP/PF/FA_1024/DP alike. Register it as amendment 06B before the FL smokes. This is the one **MUST** in §D that changes a run.
- μ = 0.01 untuned: acceptable; Li et al. 2020 sweep {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1}. A 1-EFE μ sensitivity is NICE.

### C.7 DP accounting assumptions (accountant.py L25–35, dp.py L1–24 — MEASURED)

- Mechanism: user-level adjacency (add/remove one client), flat L2 clip S, Gaussian N(0, z²S²) on the sum, fixed denominator m = 1,024, T = 384, δ = 5e-6 < 1/N, RDP over MTZ-2019 orders, standard conversion, z rounded *up* (safe side). Reviewer confirmed z by mpmath (DL 119). **Correct and conservative.**
- **Assumption 1 — Poisson bound for fixed-size WOR sampling.** Disclosed as "common practice, not a proof". Wang–Balle–Kasiviswanathan 2019 (AISTATS) provide the tight RDP bound for subsampling *without replacement* at rate γ = m/N; at the same z the WOR bound typically gives a somewhat *larger* ε for replace-one adjacency. **Recommendation (CPU-only, no re-run):** compute ε_WOR at the registered z and report both ("ε = 8.0 under the Poisson bound; ε_WOR = x under Wang et al. 2019"). If you prefer one number, keep the registered one and add the sensitivity in an appendix. This removes an easy examiner point.
- **Assumption 2 — S from an unclipped non-private pass** (median of client-delta norms over rounds 1–20 of an FA_1024-config run). Standard in DP-FedAvg practice (McMahan 2018 used fixed S; adaptive clipping of Andrew et al. 2021 is the private alternative). Disclose "S is a non-private hyper-parameter chosen from the same data; its selection is not accounted". Fine at MSc level.
- **Assumption 3 — drop-out not credited; no secure aggregation; server sees clipped deltas.** All disclosed (accountant docstring). Fine.
- **Expectation (INFERENCE, to pre-empt):** dense noise over 20.7 M coordinates with m = 1,024 and z = 0.5642 gives noise norm ≈ zS√d/m ≈ 2.5 S per round relative to a signal whose norm is ≤ m·S but in practice far smaller; ε = 8 may already lose a lot, and ε = 1 (z 1.3094) more. A large DP loss is a legitimate finding *if* the ladder is presented; it becomes a weakness if the thesis has only the ε = 8 point. Hence ε = 1 is core, not optional (§D-3).

## G. Final verdict

The project is on track to answer the proposal's question convincingly at MSc level: the retention design (arm / C_BIG with paired user-bootstrap CIs, on a global chronological split, full-catalogue exact ranking, pre-registered and sealed) is methodologically above the federated-recommendation literature it will be examined against, and every proposal step is either implemented, planned with registered rules, or dropped with a stated reason. Nothing already run needs re-running. The three changes that matter most are: (1) **pre-register and run a 2-point local-LR screen for the federated arms before any FL value is read** — the only cheap thing that can make the core number wrong; (2) **run C_FULL (one seed) and DP ε = 1 before ALL_METHODS_LOCK**, so the thesis reports retention against a real cloud reference and a privacy *curve* rather than a point; (3) **write the privacy-level ladder and the confirmatory contrast list into the lock and the thesis**, so "how much quality is retained" is always read against "at which privacy level", and the hundreds of descriptive CIs cannot be mistaken for confirmatory claims. With those, plus the disclosures already registered in amendment 06A, the thesis can defend a clear, honest answer; without (1) and (2) it answers a narrower question than the proposal asks.
